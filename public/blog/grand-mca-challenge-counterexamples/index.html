Proximity gaps vs. CA vs. MCA

Proximity gaps. A line either has every point \(\delta\)-close to \(\mathcal C\), or only an \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pg}}(\mathcal C,\delta)\)-fraction of its scalars are \(\delta\)-close.

Correlated agreement (CA). If sufficiently many scalars are \(\delta\)-close, then \(f_0\) and \(f_1\) have one common large agreement support.

Mutual correlated agreement (MCA). Outside an \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mca}}(\mathcal C,\delta)\)-fraction of scalars, every large agreement support of \(f_\lambda\) is already a common support of \(f_0\) and \(f_1\).

Grand MCA Challenge

\[ f_\lambda=f_0+\lambda f_1, \qquad f_0,f_1\in\mathbb F^n, \qquad \Delta(f,\mathcal C):=\min_{c\in\mathcal C}\Delta(f,c), \]

\[ \operatorname{Close}_\delta(f) \iff \exists c\in\mathcal C:\Delta(f,c)\leq\delta. \]

Proximity gaps

\[ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{pg}}(\mathcal C,\delta) := \max_{\substack{ f_0,f_1\in\mathbb F^n\\ \exists\lambda_0\in\mathbb F: \Delta(f_{\lambda_0},\mathcal C)>\delta }} \Pr_{\lambda\leftarrow\mathbb F} \left[ \Delta(f_\lambda,\mathcal C)\leq\delta \right]. \]

Correlated agreement

\[ \operatorname{Corr}_\delta(f_0,f_1) \iff \begin{gathered} \exists S\subseteq[n],\quad |S|\geq(1-\delta)n,\quad \exists c_0,c_1\in\mathcal C:\\ f_0|_S=c_0|_S \quad\text{and}\quad f_1|_S=c_1|_S. \end{gathered} \]

\[ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ca}}(\mathcal C,\delta) := \max_{\substack{ f_0,f_1\in\mathbb F^n\\ \neg\operatorname{Corr}_\delta(f_0,f_1) }} \Pr_{\lambda\leftarrow\mathbb F} \left[ \Delta(f_\lambda,\mathcal C)\leq\delta \right]. \]

Mutual correlated agreement

\[ \operatorname{Bad}_{\mathrm{mca}}(f_0,f_1,\lambda,\delta) \iff \begin{gathered} \exists S\subseteq[n],\quad |S|\geq(1-\delta)n,\quad \exists c\in\mathcal C:\ f_\lambda|_S=c|_S,\\ \nexists c_0,c_1\in\mathcal C: \quad f_0|_S=c_0|_S \ \land\ f_1|_S=c_1|_S. \end{gathered} \]

\[ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{mca}}(\mathcal C,\delta) := \max_{f_0,f_1\in\mathbb F^n} \Pr_{\lambda\leftarrow\mathbb F} \left[ \operatorname{Bad}_{\mathrm{mca}}(f_0,f_1,\lambda,\delta) \right]. \]

\[ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{pg}}(\mathcal C,\delta) \leq \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ca}}(\mathcal C,\delta) \leq \varepsilon_{\mathrm{mca}}(\mathcal C,\delta). \]

The grand MCA challenge (ABF 2026; Proximity Prize). We are given a Reed–Solomon code \(\mathcal C:=\operatorname{RS}[\mathbb F,\mathcal L,k]\) defined over some smooth evaluation domain \(\mathcal L\subseteq\mathbb F\). The code has constant rate, and in particular the rate \(\rho(\mathcal C):=k/|\mathcal L|\) is one of \(\left\{\frac12,\frac14,\frac18,\frac1{16}\right\}\). For a given \(\varepsilon^*\), say \(\varepsilon^*=2^{-128}\), determine the largest \(\delta_{\mathcal C}^*\in[0,1]\) such that \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mca}}(\mathcal C,\delta_{\mathcal C}^*)\leq\varepsilon^*\), assuming \(|\mathbb F|\) is sufficiently large so that such a \(\delta_{\mathcal C}^*\) exists.

Locator Polynomial Mechanisms to attack Correlated Agreement (KKH 2026; Kam 2026)

Step 0. Let’s fix concrete parameters

Say we work over

\[ \mathbb F=\mathbb F_{13}, \qquad \mathcal L=\mathbb F_{13}^{\times}=\{1,\ldots,12\}, \qquad k=3. \]

\[ \mathcal C = \operatorname{RS}(\mathbb F_{13},\mathcal L,3) = \left\{\operatorname{ev}_{\mathcal L}(p):\deg p<3\right\}. \]

Step 1. The first trick is to move to a smaller domain

Consider

\[ \pi:\mathcal L\longrightarrow G, \qquad \pi(x)=x^3, \qquad G=\pi(\mathcal L)=\{1,5,8,12\}. \]

Every point of \(G\) has exactly three preimages:

\[ \begin{array}{c|cccc} a&1&5&8&12\\ \hline \pi^{-1}(a) &\{1,3,9\} &\{7,8,11\} &\{2,5,6\} &\{4,10,12\}. \end{array} \]

Step 2. Compute every locator of fixed-size subsets

Fix \(r=2\). For every \(S\in\binom{G}{2}\), define

\[ L_S(Y) = \prod_{a\in S}(Y-a) = Y^2-e_1(S)Y+e_2(S), \]

where all coefficients are in \(\mathbb F_{13}\). The six possibilities are

\[ \begin{array}{c|c|c|c} S&e_1(S)&e_2(S)&L_S(Y)\\ \hline \{1,5\}&6&5&Y^2-6Y+5\\ \{1,8\}&9&8&Y^2-9Y+8\\ \{1,12\}&0&12&Y^2+12\\ \{5,8\}&0&1&Y^2+1\\ \{5,12\}&4&8&Y^2-4Y+8\\ \{8,12\}&7&5&Y^2-7Y+5. \end{array} \]

Every locator splits into a point on one affine line and a constant:

\[ L_S(Y) = \underbrace{Y^2-e_1(S)Y}_{u_{e_1(S)}(Y)} - \underbrace{\bigl(-e_2(S)\bigr)}_{\text{constant codeword}}. \]

Step 3. Putting everything together

Write

\[ E_1(G,2) := \left\{e_1(S):S\in\binom{G}{2}\right\} = \{0,4,6,7,9\}. \]

For every \(\lambda\in E_1(G,2)\), choose an \(S\) with \(e_1(S)=\lambda\) and set

\[ u_\lambda(Y)=Y^2-\lambda Y, \qquad c_S(Y)=-e_2(S). \]

Since \(c_S\) is constant, it is a codeword polynomial. Since every \(a\in S\) is a root of \(L_S\),

\[ 0=L_S(a) = u_\lambda(a)-c_S(a), \]

and therefore

\[ S\subseteq \operatorname{Agree}(u_\lambda,c_S). \]

Thus five distinct scalars give points on the line

\[ \left\{Y^2-\lambda Y:\lambda\in\mathbb F_{13}\right\} \]

with agreement on \(|S|/|G|=1/2\) of the small domain.

There is still one fatal issue: if we use this line directly over \(\mathcal L\), then both basis polynomials \(Y^2\) and \(-Y\) have degree below \(k=3\). They are themselves codeword polynomials, so correlated agreement holds trivially on all of \(\mathcal L\). Inflation removes this trivial common explanation while preserving the agreement fraction.

Step 4. Inflation makes the construction work

Substitute \(Y=X^3\):

\[ L_S(X^3) = \underbrace{X^6-e_1(S)X^3}_{f_{e_1(S)}(X)} - \underbrace{\bigl(-e_2(S)\bigr)}_{c_S(X)}. \]

From here on, identify each polynomial with its evaluation word on \(\mathcal L\). The ambient affine line is now

\[ f_\lambda(X) = \underbrace{X^6}_{f_0(X)} + \lambda\underbrace{(-X^3)}_{f_1(X)}. \]

Neither \(f_0\) nor \(f_1\) has degree below \(k=3\). Meanwhile, the codeword part \(c_S=-e_2(S)\) remains constant.

For each \(\lambda=e_1(S)\),

\[ L_S(x^3)=0 \iff x^3\in S \iff x\in\pi^{-1}(S) \Longrightarrow f_\lambda(x)=c_S(x). \]

Because \(\pi\) is three-to-one,

\[ |\pi^{-1}(S)|=3|S|=6, \qquad \frac{|\pi^{-1}(S)|}{|\mathcal L|} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{|S|}{|G|} = \frac{2}{4}. \]

Hence every \(\lambda\in E_1(G,2)\) satisfies

\[ \Delta(f_\lambda,\mathcal C)\leq\frac12. \]

But \(f_0\) and \(f_1\) cannot have correlated agreement on six coordinates. Indeed, for every polynomial \(p_1\) with \(\deg p_1<3\),

\[ f_1(X)-p_1(X)=-X^3-p_1(X) \]

is a nonzero polynomial of degree \(3\), so it has at most three roots. Therefore

\[ \neg\operatorname{Corr}_{1/2}(f_0,f_1). \]

We have produced a genuine correlated-agreement counterexample:

\[ \Pr_{\lambda\leftarrow\mathbb F_{13}} \left[\Delta(f_\lambda,\mathcal C)\leq\frac12\right] \geq \frac{|E_1(G,2)|}{13} = \frac5{13}, \]

and consequently

\[ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ca}}\!\left(\mathcal C,\frac12\right) \geq \frac5{13}. \]

The general mechanism

Suppose \(x\mapsto x^m\) maps \(\mathcal L\) onto \(G\) exactly \(m\)-to-one, and choose an \(r\)-subset \(S\subseteq G\). Then

\[ L_S(X^m) = \underbrace{X^{rm}-e_1(S)X^{(r-1)m}}_{f_{e_1(S)}(X)} + \underbrace{R_S(X^m)}_{\deg\leq(r-2)m}. \]

Choose the code cutoff so that

\[ (r-2)m<k\leq(r-1)m. \]

The first inequality makes \(-R_S(X^m)\) a codeword polynomial. The second pushes the line direction \(-X^{(r-1)m}\) outside the code and bounds its agreement with every codeword by \((r-1)m\) coordinates.

At the same time,

\[ \frac{|\pi^{-1}(S)|}{|\mathcal L|} = \frac{|S|}{|G|}. \]

Since \(rm>(r-1)m\), the pair cannot have correlated agreement on a support as large as \(\pi^{-1}(S)\). Thus, with

\[ \delta=1-\frac{r}{|G|}, \qquad E_1(G,r) = \left\{e_1(S):S\in\binom{G}{r}\right\}, \]

the construction gives

\[ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ca}}(\mathcal C,\delta) \geq \frac{|E_1(G,r)|}{|\mathbb F|}. \]

The problem solved by KKH is to explicitly choose \(G\) and \(r\), for suitably large generic primes, so that \(E_1(G,r)\) is large.

Concretely, for \(1\leq r\leq |G|/2\), they pair the elements of \(G\) as \(\{a,-a\}\), choose \(r\) distinct pairs, and select one signed representative from each pair. For suitably large primes, these choices give distinct sums, yielding

\[ |E_1(G,r)| \geq 2^r\binom{|G|/2}{r}. \]

After inflation, every selected \(a\in G\) represents the full coset \(\pi^{-1}(a)\) of \(\ker(\pi)\) inside \(\mathcal L\).

Extension: the higher-moment problem

The full locator expansion is

\[ L_S(X^m) = \sum_{j=0}^{r} (-1)^j e_j(S)X^{(r-j)m}, \qquad e_0(S)=1. \]

Suppose we lower the code cutoff so that the first \(t+1\) terms remain outside the code:

\[ (r-t-1)m<k\leq(r-t)m. \]

Then

\[ L_S(X^m) = \underbrace{ X^{rm} -e_1(S)X^{(r-1)m} +e_2(S)X^{(r-2)m} -\cdots+ (-1)^t e_t(S)X^{(r-t)m} }_{\text{ambient point}} + \underbrace{R_{S,t}(X^m)}_{\deg\leq(r-t-1)m}. \]

Fix values \(\beta_2,\ldots,\beta_t\) and consider the common fiber

\[ \mathcal S_{\boldsymbol\beta} := \left\{ S\in\binom{G}{r}: e_j(S)=\beta_j \text{ for }2\leq j\leq t \right\}. \]

Across this fiber, every coefficient except \(e_1(S)\) is fixed. Hence the ambient points lie on the single affine line

\[ f_\lambda(X) = \underbrace{ X^{rm} +\sum_{j=2}^{t} (-1)^j\beta_jX^{(r-j)m} }_{f_0(X)} + \lambda\underbrace{\left(-X^{(r-1)m}\right)}_{f_1(X)}, \qquad \lambda=e_1(S). \]

Define the first-moment image of this fiber by

\[ E_1(G,r;\boldsymbol\beta) := \left\{ e_1(S):S\in\mathcal S_{\boldsymbol\beta} \right\}. \]

Exactly as before, each \(\lambda\in E_1(G,r;\boldsymbol\beta)\) gives a point close to \(\mathcal C\) on the lifted support \(\pi^{-1}(S)\), while the direction \(f_1\) prevents correlated agreement on a support that large. Therefore

\[ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ca}}(\mathcal C,\delta) \geq \frac{|E_1(G,r;\boldsymbol\beta)|}{|\mathbb F|}, \qquad \delta=1-\frac{r}{|G|}. \]

What gap does this give? Write \(s=|G|\) and \(n=m s\). Since \(\rho=k/n\) and \(\delta=1-r/s\),

\[ (1-\rho)-\delta = \frac{r}{s}-\frac{k}{ms}. \]

Under the cutoff \((r-t-1)m<k\leq(r-t)m\), this lies in the interval

\[ \frac{t}{|G|} \leq (1-\rho)-\delta < \frac{t+1}{|G|}. \]

At the natural endpoint \(k=(r-t)m\), the parameters are exactly

\[ \rho=\frac{r-t}{|G|}, \qquad \delta=1-\frac{r}{|G|} =(1-\rho)-\frac{t}{|G|}. \]

Thus the naïve higher-moment construction gives a bad CA line \(t/|G|\) below capacity. The case \(t=1\) recovers the KKH gap \(1/|G|\) below capacity.

Higher-moment problem. Can we find a large fiber on which \(e_2(S),\ldots,e_t(S)\) are fixed while \(e_1(S)\) still takes many distinct values?
